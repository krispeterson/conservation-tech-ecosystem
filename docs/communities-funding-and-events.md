# Communities, Funding, and Events

## Communities and forums

### Broad conservation-tech communities

- **WILDLABS**  
  Global conservation technology community with discussions, groups, learning resources, events, and discovery tools.  
  <https://wildlabs.net/>

- **WILDLABS Open Source Solutions group**  
  A useful place to find discussion of low-cost hardware, open-source software, and practical field tools.  
  <https://wildlabs.net/groups/open-source-solutions>

- **WILDLABS Marine Conservation group**  
  Marine-focused discussions spanning bioacoustics, biologging, drones, remote sensing, and machine learning.  
  <https://wildlabs.net/groups/marine-conservation>

- **WildHub**  
  Peer-led community for conservation professionals. Not narrowly technical, but very relevant for implementation, peer support, and field realities.  
  <https://wildhub.community/>

- **GOSH**  
  Open science hardware community that matters for conservation sensors, field devices, repairability, and documentation culture.  
  <https://openhardware.science/>

## Open-source innovation hubs

These are worth calling out separately from general communities and from single repositories.

They matter because they repeatedly generate new tools, field methods, collaborations, and implementation pathways rather than just maintaining one product.

### WILDLABS

- **What it is**  
  The strongest cross-sector discovery and convening layer for conservation technology, with groups, discussions, events, courses, and The Inventory.  
  <https://wildlabs.net/>  
  <https://wildlabs.net/groups/open-source-solutions>  
  <https://wildlabs.net/inventory>

- **Best for**  
  Validating whether a problem is already being worked on, finding practitioners with similar field constraints, and recruiting early collaborators or pilot users.

- **Most actionable move**  
  Before building something new, search discussions and The Inventory, then post a concrete workflow question in the Open Source Solutions group to test whether the pain point is real and shared.

### GOSH

- **What it is**  
  Global open science hardware community with events, a forum, a roadmap, community projects, and collaborative development programs.  
  <https://openhardware.science/>  
  <https://forum.openhardware.science/>  
  <https://projects.openhardware.science/>

- **Best for**  
  Open sensor teams, device builders, and anyone trying to improve repairability, documentation quality, community governance, or regional manufacturing pathways.

- **Most actionable move**  
  Use GOSH as a design-review layer for hardware projects: compare documentation practices, ask for feedback on repairability and sourcing, and look for adjacent projects instead of reinventing enclosures, power systems, or build documentation.

### Open Acoustic Devices / AudioMoth

- **What it is**  
  A product-centered open hardware hub with supporting software, firmware, app tooling, application notes, and a support forum around the AudioMoth ecosystem.  
  <https://www.openacousticdevices.info/>  
  <https://www.openacousticdevices.info/support-forum>  
  <https://github.com/OpenAcousticDevices>

- **Best for**  
  Bioacoustics teams that want a mature, low-cost open hardware starting point with a clearer path from device setup to deployment support than most small sensor projects provide.

- **Most actionable move**  
  Treat the ecosystem as more than a recorder. Review the firmware, configuration tools, and application notes together so you can decide whether you need stock AudioMoth, alternative firmware, or a derivative workflow built on the same hardware/software boundary.

### Open Foris

- **What it is**  
  One of the clearest platform-centered open-source hubs in the sector, spanning field data collection, visual interpretation, geospatial analysis, and integrated land and restoration workflows.  
  <https://openforis.org/>  
  <https://www.openforis.org/openforis_portfolio/>  
  <https://www.openforis.org/support-and-resources/>

- **Best for**  
  Teams that need a real operational stack, not just a repo to fork, especially in forest monitoring, land-use assessment, restoration, and national or NGO implementation programs.

- **Most actionable move**  
  Treat Open Foris as a reference architecture. Map your workflow against its portfolio to decide whether you should adopt an existing component, integrate with one, or copy its packaging and support model.

### Wild Me / Wildbook

- **What it is**  
  Open software and AI ecosystem for wildlife research, identification, long-term curation, and collaboration across study sites.  
  <https://www.wildme.org/>  
  <https://community.wildme.org/>  
  <https://wildbook.docs.wildme.org/>  
  <https://github.com/WildMeOrg>

- **Best for**  
  Builders working on image-based wildlife identification, research collaboration platforms, or species-specific deployments that need both software and a community of practice.

- **Most actionable move**  
  Do not just read the code. Start from the docs and community, then work backward to the repositories so you understand deployment patterns, species-specific fit, and where extension work is more useful than a parallel rebuild.

### Digital Naturalism Laboratories / Mothbox

- **What it is**  
  Small but important maker-style hub for open field devices and low-cost biodiversity monitoring workflows, especially around Mothbox and related hardware spinoffs.  
  <https://github.com/Digital-Naturalism-Laboratories>  
  <https://digital-naturalism-laboratories.github.io/Mothbox/>

- **Best for**  
  Rapid experimentation, low-cost field instrumentation, adaptation of open designs to local contexts, and learning from highly practical build documentation.

- **Most actionable move**  
  Watch the organization, not just the flagship repo. The recent split across hardware, electronics, firmware, and processing repos is exactly the kind of signal that tells you where reuse and contribution may be easiest.

### Conservation Technology Lab

- **What it is**  
  Organization-level R&D hub at the San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance with public repositories spanning ecological ML, bioacoustics, and edge-AI monitoring.  
  <https://github.com/conservationtechlab>

- **Best for**  
  Teams looking for examples of conservation-specific AI tooling that sit closer to applied field workflows than generic academic prototypes.

- **Most actionable move**  
  Track the organization for reusable building blocks such as `animl`, `whoot`, and `tinyscrubcam`, then evaluate whether your need is a library contribution, a workflow integration, or a field deployment pattern you can adapt.

### Conservation X Labs

- **What it is**  
  More of an open-innovation hub than a classic software maintainer community, but still highly relevant because it connects conservation problems, entrepreneurs, prizes, and platform programs such as Wild Me.  
  <https://www.conservationxlabs.com/>

- **Best for**  
  Teams that need challenge-based visibility, commercialization pathways, investor or donor attention, or a bridge between prototype-stage technology and real deployment partners.

- **Most actionable move**  
  Use Conservation X Labs when the bottleneck is not code discovery but traction: partner search, problem framing, challenge participation, and translating a prototype into something adoptable.

## Why this matters

A lot of the real leverage in conservation technology does not come from a single app.

It comes from the combination of:

- communities that help people find one another
- prizes that create early traction
- implementation alliances that reduce fragmentation
- shared forums where people solve practical deployment problems

That is why these sections belong in the repo instead of being treated as side notes.
